# Problem Statement

## Background

India is one of the world's most traffic-congested countries. According to the TomTom Traffic Index, cities like Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Delhi consistently rank among the top 10 most congested globally. A core cause is low vehicle occupancy: the average car in an Indian city carries just 1.2 passengers during peak commute hours, yet roads, fuel, and parking costs scale linearly with vehicle count, not passengers.

Public transport — where it exists — is overcrowded, unreliable, and does not serve last-mile connectivity. Ride-hailing services (Ola, Uber) solve the availability problem but are prohibitively expensive for daily use and do nothing to reduce the number of vehicles on the road.

## The Problem

Working professionals and students who commute the same route daily have no structured way to coordinate with verified co-travellers. They either:
- Drive alone, paying full fuel, toll, and parking costs
- Rely on overcrowded public transport with unreliable schedules
- Use expensive ride-hailing for every trip

Informal WhatsApp groups and bulletin boards exist in some housing societies and offices, but they offer no booking flow, no driver verification, no real-time location sharing, and no city-level oversight.

The gap is not demand — it is infrastructure. There is no trusted, purpose-built platform that lets a driver say "I'm going from Andheri to BKC at 9 AM with 2 free seats" and instantly connect with verified, nearby passengers heading the same way.

## Who Is Affected

- **Daily commuters** (office workers, college students) who repeat the same route 5 days a week and bear rising fuel costs
- **Drivers** who would willingly share their car but have no safe, verified way to find passengers
- **City administrations** that cannot monitor, respond to, or improve informal ride-sharing arrangements — and have no visibility into safety incidents

## Why It Matters

- Average Indian professional spends ₹4,000–8,000/month on daily commute (KPMG Urban Mobility Report, 2023)
- Carpooling even 3 days a week can cut that cost by 40–60%
- A full car instead of a solo driver reduces per-passenger carbon emissions by up to 75%
- Safety in informal shared rides is a persistent concern, especially for women commuters — a verified driver system with SOS and city-staff oversight directly addresses this

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

| Solution | Why it falls short |
|---|---|
| **Ola/Uber Pool** | Discontinued or unreliable; treats carpooling as a discounted taxi, not a peer-to-peer match. No fixed-route recurring sharing. |
| **WhatsApp groups** | No booking flow, no verification, no accountability, no real-time tracking. Organisationally chaotic at scale. |
| **BlaBlaCar** | Designed for intercity travel (100 km+), not daily intra-city commutes. Not available in India. |
| **Public transport apps** | Show bus/metro schedules; have no mechanism for private vehicle sharing. |

CoPassage is built specifically for the **intra-city, recurring daily commute** use case — with driver verification, a passenger broadcast system, live location sharing, and city-staff safety oversight baked in from the ground up.
