# Problem Statement

## The Audience
Urban commuters, particularly in Indian cities, who rely on auto-rickshaws for their daily travel to offices, universities, or transit hubs.

## The Problem
Auto-rickshaws are a staple of urban mobility, but they are often hailed individually. A single commuter paying the full metered fare for a three-seater vehicle is highly inefficient. While existing ride-hailing apps (Uber, Ola, Rapido) offer driver dispatch services, they do not facilitate spontaneous, peer-to-peer fare splitting for autos that are hailed offline on the street.

## Quantified Pain
- **Cost**: Commuters spend 100% of the fare when they could easily be paying 33% or 50% if they had a way to find someone heading in the same direction.
- **Congestion**: 3 commuters taking 3 separate auto-rickshaws contributes to traffic congestion and triples the carbon footprint for the same route.
- **Friction**: Current "carpooling" apps focus heavily on car owners scheduling long trips. They fail to address the immediate, short-distance, offline nature of auto-rickshaw commuting.

## Why This Matters Now
With rising fuel costs and worsening urban traffic, there is an urgent need for sustainable, community-driven transit solutions. By removing the driver from the software loop and focusing entirely on commuter-to-commuter coordination, CoPassage introduces a zero-friction, highly scalable model for everyday fare splitting.
